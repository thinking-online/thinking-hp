Mind-Wandering: Lost in Thought

Rodin’s sculpture The Thinker represents a man absorbed in thought. People may wonder if he is thinking about life’s deep questions. Likewise, teachers may wonder what students sitting at their desks are thinking about―are they focusing on their schoolwork, or are they just daydreaming?

It is natural for the mind to switch between periods of focus and periods of rest. Even when we are supposed to be concentrating, we may not be paying attention to the task at hand. Scientists have found that we engage in daydreaming, also known as “mind-wandering,” approximately 50% of the time we are awake. While our minds are wandering, we are not thinking about anything special but our brains naturally come up with ideas, images, and emotions. In short, mind-wandering is a shift away from thoughts on the current task to other unrelated thoughts.

Our understanding about mind-wandering has grown over time. Past studies argued that mind-wandering was not merely a waste of time but actually quite harmful. Mind-wandering was found to negatively influence our mood, leading to work-related mistakes, traffic accidents, and even mental conditions such as depression. New research, however, shows that mind-wandering also has positive effects which can enrich our lives in various ways.

One such effect is that mind-wandering helps us generate creative ideas. In fact, some of our best ideas become apparent when our minds are wandering. To access these ideas, however, our brains sometimes require a period of time, just like eggs need time to hatch. This is called the “incubation period.” This interval away from the problem is critical because during that time our brains are unconsciously sorting through pieces of information and associating them with the problem. This enables us to come up with a fresh solution, which appears when we least expect it, for example, when we are on a walk or taking a shower.

What is really going on in the brain when the mind wanders? Previously, the brain was thought to be in a passive state at this time. However, through neuroimaging research conducted in 2001 in the US, more than a dozen regions of the brain were found to be active during mind-wandering. These regions, as a whole, were named the Default Mode Network (DMN). Moreover, according to a study conducted in 2022 by researchers based in Norway and the Netherlands, the DMN was more active when participants were performing a low-effort task compared to a high-effort task.

How can we make use of new discoveries in brain science and increase the number of unique benefits that mind-wandering has to offer? Here are four tips to help expand your mind. First, make time for simple activities such as doing the dishes or taking a warm bath. When involved with these undemanding tasks, our mind is free to wander around. Next, we can just go outside. For example, when we are in a forest, we are able to relax in nature and our mind can more easily spread out in all directions. Another suggestion is to get enough quality sleep each night. This will not only improve our mood, but enable the brain to be more receptive to new ideas. Finally, when we wake up in the morning, we should try to notice any thoughts that occur before we get out of bed. In this half-sleep state, our mind tends to wander and our ideas can flow more freely.

We will not always be able to control all of our thoughts. However, if we are aware of when and how mind-wandering happens, we may be able to take advantage of its seemingly random nature.

STEP 1

可視化リーディング ― 説明文読解の5STEP

The eye path

第7問で点が分かれるのは、英語力ではなく「読む場所を決めてから読んでいるか」です。目の動かし方を5つに固定します。1〜6で鍛えた動作（骨を取る・つまりに変換・言い換え・根拠を取り切る）を、長文でそのまま使います。

1

リード文から「自分は何を作っているのか」をつかむ

第7問のリード文は短く、場面設定も軽い。確認するのは2つだけ。自分は何をしている？ 完成させる資料は何？（概要／スライド／メモ）。答えの根拠はほぼ無いので、深く読まない。

鉄則：リード文は10秒。「何を作るか」だけ確認して、資料へ進む。
2

概要・スライドを「本文の地図」として分析する

本文より先に、概要・スライドを見る。タイトルと見出しを並べる（見出しの順≒本文の順）。空所の見出しと前後から「ここにどんな情報が入るか」を決める（穴の形）。すでに埋まっている項目は本文の内容そのもので、本文のどこに対応するかの見当になる。

鉄則：本文より先に、概要を読む。空所の形を決めてから、本文に入る。
3

設問を「何を探す問題か」に翻訳する

第7問の設問は4つの型しかない。削除型（概要にあって本文に無いもの）／図・表型（定義を条件に変換）／穴埋め型（1つ・組み合わせ）／見出し型（項目の共通点を抽象化）。設問の動詞（remove / figure / heading）で型が決まり、型で探し方が決まる。

鉄則：設問は「型」で読む。型が決まれば、探し方が決まる。
4

本文を「濃淡」で読む。該当箇所だけを精読する

段落の1文目は必ず読んで役割をつかむ。設問に関係ない段落は1文目だけでサラッと通過。地図と一致する段落に入ったら精読モードに切り替え、1文ずつ骨を取って「つまり？」に変換する。濃淡は飛ばし読みではない。該当箇所は1文ずつ訳せるレベルで読む。

鉄則：段落1文目で役割をつかみ、該当箇所だけ精読する。精読の出口は「つまり？」。
5

言い換えを照合し、根拠を取り切って確定する

正解は本文の表現が言い換えられている。不正解は本文の単語をそのまま使って、因果・主語・条件のどれか1つを変えてある。だから本文の単語と一致する選択肢ほど疑う。不正解は4つの基準で切る：因果が逆・結果がズレ／段落が違う／本文の語の流用／抽象度がズレ。

鉄則：本文の単語を探すな。「つまり」の形が一致する選択肢を探せ。
この手順の急所STEP 2 で地図ができているか。地図があれば、設問を解くときに「本文のこのあたり」に一直線で飛べる。無ければ、5問すべてで本文を頭から探し直すことになり、時間が消えます。

第1段落｜導入

Rodin’s sculpture The Thinker represents a man absorbed in thought. People may wonder if he is thinking about life’s deep questions. Likewise, teachers may wonder what students sitting at their desks are thinking about―are they focusing on their schoolwork, or are they just daydreaming?

ロダンの彫刻「考える人」は、思考に没頭する男を表している。人生の深い問いを考えているのかと思うかもしれない。同じように、教師は机に座る生徒が何を考えているのか気になるだろう。勉強に集中しているのか、ただぼんやりしているのか？

第2段落｜定義

It is natural for the mind to switch between periods of focus and periods of rest. Even when we are supposed to be concentrating, we may not be paying attention to the task at hand. Scientists have found that we engage in daydreaming, also known as “mind-wandering,” approximately 50% of the time we are awake. While our minds are wandering, we are not thinking about anything special but our brains naturally come up with ideas, images, and emotions. In short, mind-wandering is a shift away from thoughts on the current task to other unrelated thoughts.

心が集中の時間と休息の時間を行き来するのは自然なことだ。集中すべきときでも、目の前の作業に注意を向けていないことがある。科学者は、私たちが起きている時間の約50%で、「マインドワンダリング」とも呼ばれる空想にふけっていると分かった。心がさまよっている間、特別なことを考えているわけではないが、脳は自然にアイデアやイメージ、感情を生み出す。要するに、マインドワンダリングとは、今の作業についての思考から、関係のない別の思考へ移ることだ。問1

第3段落｜マイナス効果

Our understanding about mind-wandering has grown over time. Past studies argued that mind-wandering was not merely a waste of time but actually quite harmful. Mind-wandering was found to negatively influence our mood, leading to work-related mistakes, traffic accidents, and even mental conditions such as depression. New research, however, shows that mind-wandering also has positive effects which can enrich our lives in various ways.

マインドワンダリングについての理解は時とともに深まってきた。過去の研究は、それが単なる時間の無駄ではなく、実はかなり有害だと主張した。気分に悪影響を与え、仕事のミス、交通事故、さらにはうつのような精神状態につながると分かったのだ。問2しかし新しい研究は、マインドワンダリングにはさまざまな形で生活を豊かにする良い効果もあると示している。

第4段落｜プラス効果と孵化期間

One such effect is that mind-wandering helps us generate creative ideas. In fact, some of our best ideas become apparent when our minds are wandering. To access these ideas, however, our brains sometimes require a period of time, just like eggs need time to hatch. This is called the “incubation period.” This interval away from the problem is critical because during that time our brains are unconsciously sorting through pieces of information and associating them with the problem. This enables us to come up with a fresh solution, which appears when we least expect it, for example, when we are on a walk or taking a shower.

そうした効果の一つは、創造的なアイデアを生み出す助けになることだ。問2実際、最高のアイデアの中には、心がさまよっているときに現れるものがある。ただし、そのアイデアに到達するには、卵が孵るのに時間がいるように、脳にも時間が必要なことがある。これは「孵化期間」と呼ばれる。問題から離れたこの時間が重要なのは、その間に脳が無意識に情報を整理し、問題と結びつけているからだ。問3これにより、散歩中やシャワー中など、最も予期しないときに新鮮な解決策が浮かぶ。

What is really going on in the brain when the mind wanders? Previously, the brain was thought to be in a passive state at this time. However, through neuroimaging research conducted in 2001 in the US, more than a dozen regions of the brain were found to be active during mind-wandering. These regions, as a whole, were named the Default Mode Network (DMN). Moreover, according to a study conducted in 2022 by researchers based in Norway and the Netherlands, the DMN was more active when participants were performing a low-effort task compared to a high-effort task.

心がさまよっているとき、脳では実際に何が起きているのか。以前は、脳は受動的な状態だと考えられていた。しかし、2001年にアメリカで行われた脳画像研究で、マインドワンダリング中に十数か所の脳の領域が活動していると分かった。問4これらの領域は全体として「デフォルト・モード・ネットワーク（DMN）」と名付けられた。さらに、ノルウェーとオランダの研究者による2022年の研究では、負荷の高い作業と比べて、負荷の低い作業をしているときの方がDMNはより活発だった。

第6段落｜4つのコツ

How can we make use of new discoveries in brain science and increase the number of unique benefits that mind-wandering has to offer? Here are four tips to help expand your mind. First, make time for simple activities such as doing the dishes or taking a warm bath. When involved with these undemanding tasks, our mind is free to wander around. Next, we can just go outside. For example, when we are in a forest, we are able to relax in nature and our mind can more easily spread out in all directions. Another suggestion is to get enough quality sleep each night. This will not only improve our mood, but enable the brain to be more receptive to new ideas. Finally, when we wake up in the morning, we should try to notice any thoughts that occur before we get out of bed. In this half-sleep state, our mind tends to wander and our ideas can flow more freely.

We will not always be able to control all of our thoughts. However, if we are aware of when and how mind-wandering happens, we may be able to take advantage of its seemingly random nature.

私たちはいつも自分の思考すべてを制御できるわけではない。しかし、マインドワンダリングがいつ、どのように起きるかを意識していれば、その一見ランダムな性質を活用できるかもしれない。
